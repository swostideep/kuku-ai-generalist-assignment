WHERE YOU STAND
  - Time check: about 4 months have passed; the usual limit is 36 months [COW-45].
  - The Code on Wages, 2019 and the other three labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025, replacing older laws such as the Payment of Wages Act, 1936. Some rules are notified separately by each state. [COW-INFORCE]
  - Wages have time limits: monthly wages are due before the 7th day of the next month; if you resign, are removed, dismissed or retrenched, wages due must be paid within 2 working days. [COW-17]
  - A claim for unpaid or short-paid wages can be filed before the authority appointed under the Code, by you, your registered trade union, or the Inspector-cum-Facilitator, generally within 3 years from when the claim arose (later only with sufficient cause). The authority aims to decide within 3 months. [COW-45]
  - Most civil claims to recover money must be filed within 3 years; the exact start date depends on the facts. Do not wait: delay can make a valid claim unenforceable. [LIMITATION]

WHAT YOU CAN DO
  1. Write to HR/management asking for the exact amount by a date, citing the wage time limits [COW-17].
  2. Send a legal notice (draft below) if there is no answer.
  3. File a claim before the wage authority under the Code on Wages through your state Labour Department [COW-45]; a trade union or Inspector-cum-Facilitator can also file for you.
  4. Keep working records; claims generally must be made within 3 years [COW-45].

GET A LAWYER / LEGAL AID BECAUSE
  - You have not mentioned any written proof yet; a lawyer can advise what else counts as evidence.
  - You mentioned a government job: government employees are often covered by separate service rules, so the wage-claim route above may not apply. Check with legal aid (15100) first.
